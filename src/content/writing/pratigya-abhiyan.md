Before I ever trained a neural network, decoded an fMRI scan, or analyzed enterprise decision architectures, my most profound education took place on ordinary concrete floors, in classrooms illuminated by sunlight through open windows, surrounded by children whose eyes sparkled with unbroken curiosity.

That education took place at **Pratigya Abhiyan**—a student-led grassroots initiative at Thapar Institute of Engineering & Technology dedicated to imparting free, holistic education to children from underprivileged and socio-economically marginalized backgrounds.

I walked in during the early months of my Bachelor’s degree as a tentative volunteer educator. Four years later, when I completed my tenure, I had the honor of serving as **Academic Head**. 

This is the chronicle of those four years—the students who changed me, the systems we built, and the principles that continue to guide everything I do.

---

### I. The First Day: Unlearning the Pedestal

Like many engineering undergraduates, I entered the classroom with a textbook mindset: that teaching was primarily an act of information transmission. I thought having strong scores in calculus and physics meant I would naturally be a capable teacher.

I was humbled within thirty minutes.

Our students—children of daily-wage laborers, migrant workers, and local vendors—did not lack cognitive aptitude. What they lacked was structural stability:
- A seventh-grader who could mentally calculate complex grocery change in seconds because he assisted his father's stall, yet panicked when asked to write an algebraic formula on a blackboard.
- A young girl whose homework was frequently stained with cooking oil because she looked after three younger siblings after school while her parents worked night shifts.

I realized quickly that before you can teach fractions or laws of motion, you have to establish **safety, dignity, and faith**. You cannot teach from a pedestal. You have to sit on the floor beside them, understand their dialect, validate their lived realities, and show them that their thoughts matter.

---

### II. Reimagining Pedagogy: From Fear to Wonder

In conventional schooling systems, underprivileged students are frequently written off as "slow" or "disinterested." In reality, they are simply victims of rote-learning intimidation. Science and mathematics had been presented to them as punishment rather than curiosity.

As volunteers, we began reinventing our teaching methodology:
1. **Contextual Mathematics**: Instead of abstract variables, we taught proportions and percentages through real-world scenarios—crafting budgets, market arithmetic, and geometric patterns found in traditional Indian textiles and architecture.
2. **Hands-on Science & Toy Laboratories**: We built simple pinhole cameras using cardboard shoe boxes, demonstrated atmospheric pressure with glasses and index cards, and explored biological structures through fallen leaves in the campus gardens.
3. **Language as Expression, Not Barrier**: We encouraged students to express scientific observations first in their native tongue (Hindi and Punjabi) before mapping them to formal English vocabulary, removing the debilitating fear of grammatical inadequacy.

The shift was magical. Children who once sat silently in the back row began raising their hands, contesting answers, and asking questions that left us volunteers scrambling to re-read our own fundamentals.

---

### III. Stepping Up: Leading as Academic Head

As my involvement deepened, I transitioned from classroom teaching to organizational and academic stewardship. Stepping into the role of **Academic Head** was both an immense privilege and a sobering logistical challenge.

Pratigya was not an ad-hoc tutoring circle; it was an educational lifeline for hundreds of students across multiple grade levels. To ensure lasting impact, we needed operational rigor:

#### 1. Designing a Tiered & Adaptive Curriculum
Every child arrived with varying foundational deficits. Placing a 12-year-old in a standard Grade-6 curriculum when they struggled with primary-level subtraction was a recipe for alienation. 
We overhauled the academic framework:
- Replaced rigid age-based grouping with **skill-based learning cohorts** for core literacy and numeracy.
- Developed modular syllabus milestones that volunteer teachers could follow consistently across semesters.

#### 2. Mentor-Volunteer Training & Culture
Every semester brought enthusiastic new college volunteers. However, passion without pedagogical sensitivity can inadvertently harm young learners. 
- We established structured orientation programs teaching new volunteers child psychology, positive reinforcement techniques, and the critical importance of consistency—because inconsistent teacher attendance breaks trust with vulnerable children.

#### 3. Holistic Development Beyond Textbooks
Academic excellence alone is not liberation. We integrated:
- **Speech & Expression Circles**: Encouraging students to recite poetry, speak about their dreams, and debate societal topics.
- **Science Fairs & Creative Showcases**: Where students built working models and presented them to university professors and campus visitors with soaring pride.
- **Mental Health & Emotional Well-Being**: Creating spaces where students could speak openly about their anxieties, domestic pressures, and aspirations.

### V. What the Grassroots Taught Me About Technology and Life

People often look at my current resume—peer-reviewed medical AI papers at MICCAI, high-dimensional subsampling in TMLR, foundation models at Yale, enterprise decision science at Accenture—and view it as detached from grassroots social work.

They could not be more mistaken. **Pratigya Abhiyan is the conscience behind every piece of code I write.**

1. **Simplicity is the Supreme Test of Understanding**: If you cannot explain the intuition of an algorithm to a bright twelve-year-old in plain language, you do not truly understand it yourself. The clarity of thought required in grassroots teaching directly shapes how I approach research formulation and technical writing.
2. **Accountability to the Vulnerable**: When I founded the *Foundation for Ethical AI in Healthcare* and published our regulatory analysis on clinical AI in India, the patients I had in mind were not corporate executives with private healthcare access—they were the families of my Pratigya students. Any technology deployed without equity and safety checks will inevitably penalize the marginalized first.
3. **Human Potential is Uniform; Opportunity is Not**: Genius is not scarce in India; access is. When you see a student who had been branded "hopeless" blossom into a confident problem-solver simply because someone took the time to listen, you lose all patience for superficial elitism.

Pratigya Abhiyan taught me that true intellect is never measured by how high you can build your ivory tower, but by how deeply your hands are willing to reach into the soil to lift others up.
